I built the app I was missing. And its website.

When I film with a GoPro, I press a button during the recording whenever something good happens. The camera marks that moment in the file: a HighLight.

For years, Quik Desktop read those marks and sorted the clips on import. GoPro dropped it in late 2024. Since then I was left scrubbing through hours of footage to find three moments.

So I made QuiX.

Plug the camera in, and QuiX puts the marked takes in a Highlights folder and the rest in Clips. Nothing else to do.

What I enjoyed solving along the way:

→ Sorting is free. The marks sit at the end of the video file. Reading about 34 KB is enough to know where a 4 GB clip goes, before copying it.

→ Over USB, a GoPro is not a disk. It brings up a network interface and answers HTTP. It had to be treated as a small server, not a USB stick.

→ Erasing the camera never happens on its own. The button only appears once every file has been found on the Mac, at the right size.

On the product side: a native macOS app, signed and notarized by Apple, with automatic updates, in English and French. And a motion-designed website to present it (that's what scrolls in the video).

It's free, no account, no ads.
